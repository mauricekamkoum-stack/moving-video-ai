## M1 — Problem and customer validation

1. Moving companies in Luxembourg need to estimate the volume of a customer's belongings before
they can prepare an accurate moving quote. This requires an employee to visit the customer's home,
inspect the furniture and boxes, and manually estimate the total volume. This process takes staff
time, costs money to send people to measure everything each time there is a relocation needed,
creates scheduling delays, and adds time for customers who want to organise an international
relocation. These difficulties may be particularly relevant in Luxembourg, where there is a high
demand for moves into, out of, and within the country. A photo-based AI tool could allow customers to
photograph their flats and automatically estimate the volume in cubic metres of all the furniture and
prepare a quote for the moving company to review. The key problem to validate is whether this
approach can reduce the time and effort required to prepare quotes while producing estimates
accurate enough for practical use.
2. There are three distinct groups to consider: customers needing a relocation, people working in a
moving company and the businesses paying for it (which are the actual moving companies).
Primary users: Moving company staff
Employees responsible for preparing moving quotes would use the AI-generated inventory and volume
estimate to assess a customer's move more quickly. They could review the result and adjust it before
sending a final quote.
Paying customers: Moving companies
Small and medium-sized moving companies operating in Luxembourg are the initial target market.
They could pay a subscription or a fee per estimate if the tool reduces staff workload and makes it
possible to handle more quote requests.
End users: People planning a move
Individuals and families moving within Luxembourg, arriving from abroad, or leaving the country could
photograph their belongings using a phone. They would benefit from such process without having to
deal with arranging a home visit.
It is also worth mentioning that moving company staff would either:
* have less work to do
* have different work to do (instead of driving to different places they would review the pictures and
possibly send feedback if any corrections were needed)
* would lose their jobs
So the money invested in company staff could be put into paying for using such a AI system.
Target segment and market size: The initial target market is small and medium-sized moving
companies operating in Luxembourg that regularly prepare household moving quotes. Individuals and
families moving into, out of, or within Luxembourg would be the end users supplying photos of their
belongings. STATEC recorded 24,985 arrivals and 17,141 international departures in Luxembourg in
2025, indicating a substantial level of cross-border population movement. These numbers are not a
direct estimate of the market for this product but more like a demand indicator. We can't accurately
state the number of companies that would be willing to buy such product without other form of
information, for example: interviews.
3.Interviews (bullet points, notes and details are below).
1st:
1- AI could reduce manual listing, but customers should be able to add missing items and correct
quantities.
2- Room-by-room filming instructions should include cellars and storage areas to reduce omissions.
3- Customers should be able to update the number of boxes as packing progresses.
4- Clear filming instructions and information about video access, storage and deletion could support
adoption.
5- Uncertain dimensions should be flagged, and the moving company should review the estimate,
access conditions and pricing before confirming the quotation.
2nd:
1- Complete inventories, dimensions and access information are essential for preparing reliable
quotations.
2- Missing items and unclear access conditions can lead to additional checks and inaccurate
estimates.
3- AI could help create an initial inventory and reduce manual information collection.
Staff would still need to verify uncertain measurements, unusual items and the final quotation.
4- Adoption would depend on demonstrated time savings, reliable results and a price justified by
those benefits.
4. Three existing alternatives:
• In-person visits by company's estimators:
Fall short because of the costs of transport (company car, petrol) and covering the worker's wage by
the company. Moreover it occupies the schedule for other visits.
• Hiring freelance estimators
High costs of visits, relying on independent workers and their limited time
• Web forms with manual customer data entry:
Cheaper than hiring workers for this job, but the company needs to be aware of the clients' errors
which can lead to the matching of the wrong truck
• 3D scanning apps:
They require the customers to perform a slow, thorough scanning proces + the apps don't itemise the
scanned furniture or calculate total transport volume
5. For moving companies who spend money on their workers wage to investigate customer's
apartments, meanwhile losing opportunities for extra income due to full schedule, our AI app is a
quoting automation tool that categorises furniture and estimates its volume from user-submitted
photos, unlike hiring workers to measure the volume themselves.
6. Why AI
AI can identify furniture and boxes in a customer’s video and turn these observations into a structured
inventory. Without AI, a moving-company employee would need to review the footage manually or
conduct an on-site visit, which takes time. The inventory supports volume estimation using reference
dimensions and helps prepare a preliminary quotation. Customers confirm detected items and
quantities, while the moving company validates the estimate before issuing the quotation.

Interview notes:
Interview 1 with an anonymous person Luxembourg who recently moved.
This interview focuses on the experience of a person who recently moved. It aims to understand how
they organised their move, obtained quotations and communicated information about their
belongings. It also explores the difficulties they encountered and their views on an AI-assisted video
tool, particularly its usefulness, ease of use and privacy implications.
QUESTIONS:
Q. Could you briefly describe your recent move? Did you use a moving company, move independently,
or combine both?
A: I moved between two apartments in Luxembourg. I packed smaller belongings myself and
hired a moving company to transport the furniture and boxes.
Q. What information did you have to provide to obtain a quotation, and how did you provide it?
A: I provided both addresses, the moving date, the floors and lift availability, along with a list of
furniture and an approximate number of boxes. I sent photos by email, and the company
contacted me to clarify some dimensions.
Q. Approximately how much time did you spend listing your belongings, providing information and
arranging assessments? Were there any related expenses?
A: I did not measure the time precisely, but I had to prepare the list, take photos and answer
follow-up questions on several occasions. There was no separate assessment fee, but it took
time to gather the information.
Q. What was the most difficult part of describing your belongings or arranging the move? Could you
give a specific example?
A: Estimating the number of boxes was difficult because I had not finished packing. I also did not
know which furniture dimensions mattered, so I had to ask the company and take additional
measurements.
Q. Were any belongings forgotten, dimensions misunderstood or access conditions overlooked? If so,
what happened?
A: I initially forgot some items stored in the cellar. After adding them, the company reviewed the
inventory and checked whether the planned transport capacity was sufficient. This showed me
how easily a written list can be incomplete.
“ Thank you for sharing your experience. You mentioned difficulties estimating box quantities,
identifying relevant measurements and remembering items in storage areas. We would now like to
hear your views on a possible approach: customers film each room and storage area, and AI proposes
an inventory that they can review and correct. The tool would then estimate an approximate volume
and help the moving company prepare a preliminary quotation. Thinking back to your move, where
might this have helped, and what concerns would you have? ”
Q. Thinking back to your move, which steps could this tool have made easier, and which would still
have required help from the moving company?
A: It could have helped me create the initial furniture inventory instead of writing everything
down. However, I would still have needed guidance on measurements and access conditions. It
would not have solved the uncertainty about the final number of boxes before packing was
finished.
Q. How would you feel about filming each room and storage area? What could make this difficult or
uncomfortable?
A: I would consider it if the filming instructions were clear. I would want to avoid recording
personal documents, valuables or other people. Before uploading, I would also want to know
who could access the video and when it would be deleted.
Q. If the tool generated an inventory, how would you check it? What would help you identify missing or
incorrectly counted items?
A: I would review the inventory room by room and compare it with my belongings. A checklist
reminding me to include the cellar and other storage areas would help. I would also want to edit
quantities, add missing items and see which dimensions needed confirmation.
Q. What would make you comfortable using the estimated volume and preliminary quotation to plan
your move?
A: I would want the moving company to review the information and explain what was included in
the quotation. Uncertain measurements and possible additional charges should be clearly
identified. I would use the preliminary quotation for planning, but I would still want confirmation
from the company before booking.

Interviewee: Jordan Samen, Owner of a moving company in Luxembourg
Date: 11 October 2026
Duration: 30 min
Interviewer: Maurice Kamkoum
“ Hello, I am a student at the University of Luxembourg. Our team is studying how moving companies
prepare quotations and exploring whether video-based artificial intelligence could make this process
easier. This interview will take approximately 20–30 minutes. May I take notes and include an
anonymised summary in our coursework? You may skip any questions if you wish.”
QUESTIONS:
Q- What is your role in the company, and are you involved in preparing moving quotations?
A : I coordinate moving requests and prepare quotations. I collect information from customers,
estimate the resources needed and discuss difficult cases with our operational team
Q- Could you walk me through how you prepared your most recent quotation?
A: The customer contacted us by email. We asked for the moving addresses, preferred date, an
inventory and access details. After reviewing the photos, we requested additional
measurements, estimated the volume and prepared the quotation.
Q- What information do you need from the customer before you can provide a quotation?
A: We need the inventory, approximate dimensions, addresses, floors, lift availability, parking
conditions and any packing or dismantling services .
Q- When do you use an on-site visit, photos, a video call or another method?
A: Photos may be sufficient for a small move. For a larger or more complicated move, we prefer a
video call or an on-site visit.
Q- How much staff time does one quotation typically require, including travel and administration?
A: A straightforward quotation can be prepared relatively quickly when the customer provides
complete information. Follow-up questions and on-site visits take longer. Travel and
administration should be included when measuring the total time.
Q- Approximately how much does preparing one quotation cost your company? An estimate or range
is fine.
A: We do not calculate a separate cost for every quotation. The main expenses are staff time and
travel. We could estimate the cost using the time spent, our internal hourly staff cost and any
travel expenses
Q- What mistakes or missing information cause the most problems? Could you give a recent
example?
A: Customers sometimes forget items in cellars or storage rooms, or provide incomplete access
information. For example, an item that does not fit in the lift can change the handling time and
equipment required.
Q- How do you currently estimate volume in cubic metres, and how do you check that estimate?
A: We estimate volume from the inventory, dimensions and our experience with similar items.
We also consider dismantling and how items can be loaded. If the information is uncertain, we
request measurements or carry out a further assessment
“ We are considering developing a tool that allows customers to upload a short video of each room.
The AI then generates an inventory of furniture and boxes, which the customer reviews and corrects.
The system then estimates the volume and prepares a preliminary quotation for the moving
company to review.”
Q- Where could this tool help your current process? Which tasks would still require staff
involvement or an on-site visit?
A- It could help us create an initial inventory and reduce the time spent asking customers to
list their belongings. Staff would still need to check unusual or fragile items, access conditions
and the final quotation. Complex moves might still require an on-site visit
Q- What errors would make the results unusable? What would you need to check before trusting the
inventory and volume estimate?
A: Missing large items, counting the same furniture twice or estimating dimensions incorrectly
could significantly affect the quotation. Before using the results, we would check that all
rooms and storage areas are covered, confirm quantities and review uncertain measurements.
The tool should clearly flag items that need verification
Q- What practical concerns could prevent your company or customers from using it?
Would your company consider paying for such a tool? Why or why not? If yes, what pricing
arrangement or range would be reasonable?
A- We would consider paying if a trial showed that the tool saves staff time without increasing
quotation errors. A payment per completed assessment could be suitable at first. A
subscription might make sense for regular use. We would need to compare the price with the
time saved and the amount of checking still required
The interview suggests that quotation preparation depends on complete inventories, reliable
dimensions and accurate access information. Missing items and uncertain measurements can
require further checks. An AI-assisted video tool could help produce an initial inventory, but staff
would still need to review uncertain items, access conditions and the final quotation. Willingness to
pay was conditional on demonstrated time savings without increased quotation errors. No specific
preparation time, cost or acceptable price was provided, so these remain to be quantified.
